Measuring Liquid Water: Simulation versus Experiment

A water model is only as good as the numbers it produces. In this last module we run a liquid-water simulation with ASE and compute, one by one, the quantities that experiments measure: the energy and heat of vaporisation, the structure, the diffusion constant, the dielectric constant and the pressure, and then we put each next to its experimental value and ask honestly how well it does.

Prerequisites Modules 8, 9 and 10 What you run 125 SPC/E molecules with Ewald electrostatics, 6 ps equilibration and 16 ps of NVE Expected time to finish About 60 minutes

What you will learn

  • How to turn a trajectory into thermodynamic, structural, dynamical and dielectric properties.
  • How to compute the heat of vaporisation and why SPC/E needs a polarisation correction.
  • How to estimate a diffusion constant from the mean-squared displacement, and why small boxes need a finite-size correction.
  • Why some properties (energy, structure) converge in picoseconds and others (dielectric constant) do not.
  • How to estimate uncertainties from independent blocks, and what a fair comparison with experiment looks like.
How to use this page

The numbers and the figures in Lessons 2–7 come from a real ASE run: the code cells show the scripts (m12_production.py and m12_analyse.py, with water_ase.py from Module 9) and everything they printed. The Interactive panel at the end reruns a much smaller version in your browser with the course’s JavaScript engine, which is why its numbers are noisier. Press P for presentation mode.

Lesson 1What Can We Measure?

A simulation produces positions and velocities. To compare with a laboratory we need properties, each of which is an average over the trajectory, computed by a formula from statistical mechanics:

PropertyHow it is computedExperiment at 298 K
Heat of vaporisation $\Delta H_\mathrm{vap}$$-\langle U\rangle/N + RT$, plus corrections44.0 kJ/mol (10.52 kcal/mol) [2]
Structure: $g_\mathrm{OO}(r)$Histogram of O···O distances (Module 8)first peak at about 2.8 Å, height about 2.5 [3, 4]
Self-diffusion $D$Slope of the mean-squared displacement, $\mathrm{MSD} = 6Dt$2.30 × 10⁻⁵ cm²/s [2, 5]
Static dielectric constant $\varepsilon$Fluctuations of the total dipole moment of the box78.5 [2]
Pressure / densityKinetic + virial; or an NPT run0.997 g/cm³ at 1 bar [2]
Hydrogen-bond numberA geometric criterion on each pairabout 3.5 donated + accepted per molecule (not directly measurable: definition-dependent)

Two points to keep in mind. First, every simulation estimate has a statistical error (how much it would change in an independent run) and often a systematic error (finite box size, cut-offs, a short run). We estimate the first from independent blocks of the trajectory. Second, even a perfect simulation of a model compares the model, not the calculation, with experiment: the SPC/E water we simulate is only an effective description of real water (Modules 9 and 10).

Lesson 2The Simulation: SPC/E in ASE

We use the rigid SPC/E model of Module 9 with the Ewald electrostatics of water_ase.py: 125 molecules in a cubic box of 15.54 Å (density 0.997 g/cm³, real-space cutoff 7.76 Å, just under half the box). The protocol:

  1. Build the box (the least clashing of 60 random orientations per molecule) and minimise it.
  2. Equilibrate for 0.4 ps with strong friction and 6 ps with a weak Langevin thermostat at 300 K.
  3. Remove the thermostat and run 16 ps in the NVE ensemble with a 2 fs time step. All properties are computed from this run, as in Rahman’s argon simulation (Module 8): dynamics measured in a thermostat-free run is the cleanest.

During the run we record, at different intervals: the box dipole every step, the oxygen positions and the energy every 10 fs, $g_\mathrm{OO}$ and the hydrogen-bond count every 40 fs, and the pressure every 200 fs. The pressure of a rigid-molecule system needs the derivative of the energy with respect to the volume. We obtain it numerically by scaling the box and the molecule centres (keeping the molecules rigid) by $1\pm 2\times10^{-4}$ and differentiating the Ewald energy with the reciprocal-lattice set held fixed, so that the energy is a smooth function of the volume.

"""Module 12: properties of liquid SPC/E water with ASE (125 molecules, Ewald, 300 K).

Protocol: minimise -> Langevin equilibration -> NVE production; everything below is computed from the NVE run.
"""
import sys, time
import numpy as np
from ase import units
from ase.md.langevin import Langevin
from ase.md.verlet import VelocityVerlet
from ase.md.velocitydistribution import MaxwellBoltzmannDistribution, Stationary
from ase.optimize import FIRE
from water_ase import Water3Site, water_box, rigid_constraint, MODELS, KE

N_MOL = int(sys.argv[1]) if len(sys.argv) > 1 else 125
N_EQ, N_PROD = (int(sys.argv[2]), int(sys.argv[3])) if len(sys.argv) > 3 else (3000, 8000)
T0, DT = 300.0, 2.0                                   # K, fs
KJ = 96.4853                                          # eV -> kJ/mol
kT = units.kB * T0

atoms = water_box("spce", N_MOL, density_gcc=0.997, seed=11)
L = atoms.cell[0, 0]; V = L ** 3
RC = min(9.0, L / 2 - 0.01)                           # real-space cutoff: 9 A, or just under half the box; pinned so that it does not change when the volume is perturbed
calc = Water3Site("spce", rc=RC); atoms.calc = calc
atoms.set_constraint(rigid_constraint(N_MOL, "spce"))
print(f"SPC/E, {N_MOL} molecules in a {L:.3f} A cube (density {N_MOL * 18.01528 / 0.602214076 / V:.3f} g/cm^3), Ewald real-space cutoff {RC:.2f} A")
FIRE(atoms, logfile=None).run(fmax=0.3, steps=500)
MaxwellBoltzmannDistribution(atoms, temperature_K=T0, rng=np.random.default_rng(3)); Stationary(atoms)
Langevin(atoms, 1.0 * units.fs, temperature_K=T0, friction=0.1 / units.fs, rng=np.random.default_rng(4)).run(400)
eq = Langevin(atoms, DT * units.fs, temperature_K=T0, friction=0.01 / units.fs, rng=np.random.default_rng(5))
t0 = time.time()
eq.attach(lambda: print(f"  equilibration {eq.nsteps * DT / 1000:5.1f} ps  Epot/N = {atoms.get_potential_energy() / N_MOL * KJ:7.2f} kJ/mol  T = {atoms.get_temperature():6.1f} K", flush=True), interval=1000)
eq.run(N_EQ)
print(f"equilibrated in {time.time() - t0:.0f} s", flush=True)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- production (NVE)
m = atoms.get_masses()[:3]; q = np.array([MODELS["spce"]["qO"], MODELS["spce"]["qH"], MODELS["spce"]["qH"]])
rec = dict(t=[], T=[], U=[], Utail=[], Opos=[], M=[], P=[], hb=[])
rmax, nb = L / 2 - 0.05, 200
hist = np.zeros(nb); nfr = 0

def box_dipole():
    return (atoms.positions.reshape(-1, 3, 3) * q[None, :, None]).sum(axis=(0, 1))          # e*A

def hbonds():
    R = atoms.positions.reshape(-1, 3, 3); O, H = R[:, 0], R[:, 1:]
    d = O[None, :, :] - O[:, None, :]; d -= L * np.round(d / L)                              # O_i -> O_j
    r = np.linalg.norm(d, axis=-1); np.fill_diagonal(r, 99)
    n = 0
    for h in range(2):
        v = H[:, h, :] - O                                                                    # O_i -> its H
        cos = (v[:, None, :] * d).sum(-1) / (np.linalg.norm(v, axis=1)[:, None] * r)
        n += ((r < 3.5) & (cos > np.cos(np.radians(30)))).sum()
    return n / N_MOL

def pressure_bar():
    """Pressure of the rigid-molecule system: kinetic (centre-of-mass) part + (-dU/dV) from rigid scaling of the molecules."""
    R = atoms.positions.reshape(-1, 3, 3); com = (R * m[None, :, None]).sum(1) / m.sum()
    pcom = (atoms.get_momenta().reshape(-1, 3, 3)).sum(1)
    ke_com = (pcom ** 2).sum() / (2 * m.sum())
    def U(lam):
        a2 = atoms.copy(); a2.set_constraint()
        a2.set_cell(atoms.cell * lam, scale_atoms=False)
        a2.set_positions((R + (lam - 1) * com[:, None, :]).reshape(-1, 3))
        a2.calc = pcalc
        return a2.get_potential_energy()
    dl = 2e-4
    dUdl = (U(1 + dl) - U(1 - dl)) / (2 * dl)
    Pc = -dUdl / (3 * V)                                   # eV/A^3
    Pk = 2 * ke_com / (3 * V)
    x3 = (MODELS["spce"]["sigma"] / calc.rc) ** 3; rho = N_MOL / V                            # analytic LJ tail of the pressure
    Ptail = 16 / 3 * np.pi * rho ** 2 * MODELS["spce"]["eps"] * MODELS["spce"]["sigma"] ** 3 * (2 / 3 * x3 ** 3 - x3)
    return (Pk + Pc + Ptail) * 1.602176634e6               # bar

# a calculator with the Ewald k-set pinned, so that U(lambda) is a smooth function of the volume
atoms.get_potential_energy(); pcalc = Water3Site("spce", rc=RC, nmax=int(np.ceil(3.03 * calc.alpha * L / np.pi)))

def save():
    np.savez("m12_out.npz", **{k: np.array(v) for k, v in rec.items()}, hist=hist, nfr=nfr, L=L)

nve = VelocityVerlet(atoms, DT * units.fs)
step = {"i": 0}
def sample():
    i = step["i"] = step["i"] + 1
    rec["M"].append(box_dipole())
    if i % 5 == 0:
        rec["t"].append(i * DT / 1000); rec["T"].append(atoms.get_temperature())
        rec["U"].append(atoms.get_potential_energy()); rec["Utail"].append(calc.tail_correction(atoms))
        rec["Opos"].append(atoms.positions.reshape(-1, 3, 3)[:, 0].copy())
    if i % 20 == 0:
        global hist, nfr
        O = atoms.positions.reshape(-1, 3, 3)[:, 0]; d = O[None] - O[:, None]; d -= L * np.round(d / L)
        r = np.linalg.norm(d, axis=-1)[np.triu_indices(N_MOL, 1)]
        hist += 2 * np.histogram(r, bins=nb, range=(0, rmax))[0]; nfr += 1
        rec["hb"].append(hbonds())
    if i % 100 == 0: rec["P"].append(pressure_bar())
    if i % 2000 == 0:
        save()                                          # checkpoint, so that an interrupted run keeps its data
        print(f"  production {i * DT / 1000:5.1f} ps  T = {np.mean(rec['T'][-400:]):6.1f} K  Epot/N = {np.mean(rec['U'][-400:]) / N_MOL * KJ:7.2f} kJ/mol  elapsed {time.time() - t0:5.0f} s", flush=True)
nve.attach(sample, interval=1)
Etot0 = atoms.get_total_energy()
nve.run(N_PROD)
print(f"NVE total-energy drift over {N_PROD * DT / 1000:.0f} ps: {(atoms.get_total_energy() - Etot0) * 1000:+.1f} meV for {N_MOL} molecules", flush=True)
save()

The log shows the energy settling at about −45.5 kJ/mol per molecule after the first 2 ps of equilibration, and the temperature near 300 K. In the NVE run the temperature is not controlled: it stayed around 300 K, averaging 304 K with ±14 K fluctuations (as expected for 125 molecules), and the total energy drifted by only 1.2 meV for the whole 125-molecule system over 16 ps (last line of the log). That confirms that the integrator, the SHAKE/RATTLE constraints and the smooth Ewald forces conserve energy. Note that the average temperature is 304 K, not the 298 K at which the experimental values below were measured; we will come back to that.

Lesson 3Energy and the Heat of Vaporisation

The heat of vaporisation is the energy needed to turn the liquid into an ideal gas of molecules at the same temperature:

$$ \Delta H_\mathrm{vap} = \langle E_\mathrm{gas}\rangle - \langle E_\mathrm{liq}\rangle/N + RT, $$

where $E_\mathrm{gas}$ is the energy of an isolated molecule and $RT$ is the work $P\Delta V = k_BT$ per molecule done when one liquid molecule becomes a gas molecule (the liquid volume is negligible). For a rigid model the gas-phase energy is zero (the intramolecular energy is constant), so $\Delta H_\mathrm{vap} = -\langle U\rangle/N + RT$. We use the energy with the full, unshifted Lennard-Jones interaction plus its analytic tail correction beyond the 9 Å cutoff, which the script stores separately from the shifted energy used for the dynamics.

That would be the end of the story for a model that was fitted to the gas as well as the liquid. SPC/E is not: its charges are larger than those of an isolated molecule so that, on average, it imitates a molecule polarised by its neighbours (Module 9). The molecules in a real liquid pay an energy price to be polarised from the gas-phase dipole $\mu_0 = 1.85$ D to the liquid-phase dipole $\mu = 2.35$ D; the simulation never charges that price, so the potential energy is too negative. Berendsen et al. [1] estimate it as $E_\mathrm{pol} = (\mu-\mu_0)^2 / 2\alpha$ with a polarisability $\alpha = 1.44$ Å³, and subtract it:

$$ \Delta H_\mathrm{vap} = -\langle U\rangle/N + RT - E_\mathrm{pol}. $$

Berendsen et al. report the SPC/E polarization correction as 5.22 kJ/mol [1]; the analysis below uses that published value.

Analysis: all properties from the stored NVE data (python m12_analyse.py m12_out.npz)
"""Module 12 analysis: turn the NVE trajectory data of m12_production.py into properties of liquid SPC/E water."""
import json, sys
import numpy as np
from ase import units

d = np.load(sys.argv[1] if len(sys.argv) > 1 else "m12_out.npz")
N = d["Opos"].shape[1]; L = float(d["L"]); V = L ** 3
T_U, U, Utail, Opos, M, P, hb = d["T"], d["U"], d["Utail"], d["Opos"], d["M"], d["P"], d["hb"]
dt_frame = 0.010                                   # ps between stored frames (5 steps of 2 fs)
n_frames = len(U); t = np.arange(n_frames) * dt_frame
kB = units.kB; Tm = T_U.mean(); kT = kB * Tm      # eV
KJ = 96.4853; RT_kJ = 8.314462618e-3 * Tm
blocks = np.array_split(np.arange(n_frames), 4)
def sem(x): return np.std(x, ddof=1) / np.sqrt(len(x))
print(f"{N} SPC/E molecules, box {L:.3f} A, NVE run of {n_frames * dt_frame:.0f} ps, <T> = {Tm:.1f} +- {T_U.std():.1f} K\n")

# ---- 1. energy and the heat of vaporisation ---------------------------------------------------------
u_mol = (U + Utail) / N * KJ                       # kJ/mol per molecule (unshifted LJ + analytic tail)
mu_liq, mu_gas, alpha_pol = 2.35, 1.85, 1.44      # D, D, A^3 (rounded SPC/E values from Berendsen et al. 1987)
E_pol = 5.22                                        # kJ/mol, correction reported by Berendsen et al. (1987)
dH_raw = [-u_mol[b].mean() + RT_kJ for b in blocks]
dH_cor = [x - E_pol for x in dH_raw]
print(f"potential energy      <U>/N          = {u_mol.mean():7.2f} kJ/mol  (blocks: {', '.join(f'{u_mol[b].mean():.2f}' for b in blocks)})")
print(f"heat of vaporisation  -<U>/N + RT    = {np.mean(dH_raw):7.2f} +- {sem(dH_raw):.2f} kJ/mol   ({np.mean(dH_raw) / 4.184:.2f} kcal/mol, before the polarisation correction)")
print(f"polarisation correction E_pol        = {E_pol:7.2f} kJ/mol  (mu = {mu_liq} D, gas-phase mu = {mu_gas} D, alpha = {alpha_pol} A^3)")
print(f"corrected             dHvap          = {np.mean(dH_cor):7.2f} +- {sem(dH_cor):.2f} kJ/mol   ({np.mean(dH_cor) / 4.184:.2f} kcal/mol)   experiment: 44.0 kJ/mol\n")

# ---- 2. structure -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
hist, nfr = d["hist"], int(d["nfr"]); nb = len(hist); rmax = L / 2 - 0.05
edges = np.linspace(0, rmax, nb + 1); r = 0.5 * (edges[1:] + edges[:-1]); rho_n = N / V
shell = 4 / 3 * np.pi * (edges[1:] ** 3 - edges[:-1] ** 3)
g = hist / (nfr * N * rho_n * shell)
k1 = int(np.argmax(g)); kmin = k1 + int(np.argmin(g[k1:k1 + 40]))
cn = np.sum(4 * np.pi * rho_n * g[:kmin + 1] * r[:kmin + 1] ** 2 * (edges[1] - edges[0]))
print(f"g_OO(r): first peak at r = {r[k1]:.2f} A, height {g[k1]:.2f}; first minimum at {r[kmin]:.2f} A (g = {g[kmin]:.2f}); coordination number {cn:.2f}")
print(f"          experiment (X-ray, 298 K): first peak near 2.80 A, height about 2.5\n")

# ---- 3. hydrogen bonds and dipole -----------------------------------------------------------------
print(f"hydrogen bonds donated per molecule (O...O < 3.5 A, H-O...O < 30 deg): {hb.mean():.2f} +- {sem([hb[b2].mean() for b2 in np.array_split(np.arange(len(hb)), 4)]):.2f}  (3.5 donated+accepted)\n")

# ---- 4. self-diffusion -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
lag_frames = np.arange(0, min(1000, n_frames // 3), max(1, int(10 * min(1.0, n_frames * dt_frame / 40.0))))             # every 0.1 ps up to ~10 ps
def msd_curve(Pseg, lags):
    return np.array([0.0 if l == 0 else np.mean(np.sum((Pseg[l:] - Pseg[:-l]) ** 2, axis=2)) for l in lags]) if len(Pseg) > max(lags) else None
msd = msd_curve(Opos, lag_frames); tl = lag_frames * dt_frame
def slope_D(tt, mm, lo, hi):
    s = (tt >= lo) & (tt <= hi); return np.polyfit(tt[s], mm[s], 1)[0] / 6.0 * 1e-4 * 1e5        # A^2/ps -> 1e-5 cm^2/s
total_ps = n_frames * dt_frame
fit_lo, fit_hi = (1.5, 5.0) if total_ps >= 12 else (0.3 * total_ps / 3, 0.9 * total_ps / 3)        # diffusive window of the MSD (shorter for tiny test runs)
D_all = slope_D(tl, msd, fit_lo, fit_hi)
n_blk = 2 if total_ps < 30 else 4; seg = n_frames // n_blk; Db = []
for i in range(n_blk):
    lg = np.arange(0, seg // 2, 10); m_i = msd_curve(Opos[i * seg:(i + 1) * seg], lg)
    Db.append(slope_D(lg * dt_frame, m_i, 1.0, 3.5) if total_ps >= 12 else slope_D(lg * dt_frame, m_i, fit_lo / 2, fit_hi / 2))
eta = 0.890e-3                                                      # Pa s, experimental viscosity of water at 25 C (used for the correction)
dD = 2.837297 * units.kB * Tm * 1.602176634e-19 / (6 * np.pi * eta * L * 1e-10) * 1e4 / 1e-5       # m^2/s -> 1e-5 cm^2/s
print(f"self-diffusion D (Einstein, MSD slope {fit_lo:.1f}-{fit_hi:.1f} ps)     = {D_all:.2f} x 1e-5 cm^2/s")
print(f"  {n_blk} independent {total_ps / n_blk:.0f} ps blocks (slope 1-3.5 ps)       = {np.mean(Db):.2f} +- {sem(Db):.2f}  ({', '.join(f'{x:.2f}' for x in Db)})")
print(f"  finite-size correction (Yeh-Hummer, eta = 0.890 mPa s): +{dD:.2f}  ->  D(infinite system) = {D_all + dD:.2f} x 1e-5 cm^2/s   experiment: 2.30\n")

# ---- 5. dielectric constant ------------------------------------------------------------------------
Mv = M[len(M) - n_frames * 5:]                                     # one dipole per step; use the same NVE run
Mvar = lambda x: ((x ** 2).sum(1).mean() - (x.mean(0) ** 2).sum())
eps_f = lambda x: 1 + 4 * np.pi * 14.3996454784 * Mvar(x) / (3 * V * kT)
eps_b = [eps_f(Mv[i * (len(Mv) // 4):(i + 1) * (len(Mv) // 4)]) for i in range(4)]
print(f"static dielectric constant (box-dipole fluctuations): {eps_f(Mv):.0f}  (four {total_ps / 4:.0f} ps blocks: {', '.join(f'{x:.0f}' for x in eps_b)}; spread {np.std(eps_b, ddof=1):.0f})")
print(f"  SPC/E literature value: 68 (Vega-Abascal Table 2);  experiment: 78.5\n")

# ---- 6. pressure -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pb = [np.mean(P[b2]) for b2 in np.array_split(np.arange(len(P)), 4)]
print(f"pressure at 0.997 g/cm^3: {np.mean(P):.0f} bar  (blocks: {', '.join(f'{x:.0f}' for x in Pb)}; sem {sem(Pb):.0f})  -> 1 bar is the equilibrium state\n")

# ---- for the web page --------------------------------------------------------------------------------
def cum(f, step_ps, stride=1):
    out = []
    for tt in np.arange(step_ps, n_frames * dt_frame + 1e-9, step_ps):
        out.append((float(tt), float(f(tt))))
    return out
dh_cum = [(tt, float(-((U + Utail)[:int(tt / dt_frame)].mean()) / N * KJ + RT_kJ - E_pol)) for tt in np.arange(1, n_frames * dt_frame + 1e-9, 1.0)]
eps_cum = [(tt, float(eps_f(Mv[:int(tt / 0.002)]))) for tt in np.arange(2, n_frames * dt_frame + 1e-9, 1.0)]
json.dump({
    "uN": float(u_mol.mean()), "dHraw": [float(np.mean(dH_raw)), float(sem(dH_raw))], "dHrawk": float(np.mean(dH_raw) / 4.184), "Epol": float(E_pol),
    "dHdev": float(abs(np.mean(dH_cor) - 44.0) / 44.0 * 100), "Dinf": float(D_all + dD),
    "N": N, "L": L, "T": float(Tm), "tPs": float(n_frames * dt_frame),
    "g": {"r": [float(x) for x in r], "g": [float(x) for x in g]},
    "msd": {"t": [float(x) for x in tl], "msd": [float(x) for x in msd]},
    "dH": [float(np.mean(dH_cor)), float(sem(dH_cor))], "dHcum": dh_cum, "epsCum": eps_cum,
    "D": [float(D_all), float(np.mean(Db)), float(sem(Db)), float(dD)], "fitWin": [float(fit_lo), float(fit_hi)], "nBlk": int(n_blk), "eps": [float(eps_f(Mv))] + [float(x) for x in eps_b],
    "P": [float(np.mean(P)), float(sem(Pb))], "hb": float(hb.mean()), "peak": [float(r[k1]), float(g[k1])], "cn": float(cn),
}, open("m12_results.json", "w"))

The average potential energy is -46.40 kJ/mol per molecule, which is what the literature gives for SPC/E (the raw heat of vaporisation that the Vega–Abascal review tabulates, 11.79 kcal/mol, corresponds to about −46.8 kJ/mol at 298 K [2]). Before the polarisation correction this gives $-\langle U\rangle/N + RT = $ 48.9 kJ/mol (11.69 kcal/mol), 11 % above the experimental 44.0 kJ/mol: the model overbinds. Subtracting the published $E_\mathrm{pol} = $ 5.22 kJ/mol gives 43.7 kJ/mol, within 0.7 % of experiment, as the model was designed to do. The block standard error is only 0.04 kJ/mol, but that reflects the statistical noise alone: the polarization correction itself is approximate, so the agreement should be read as “within about 1–2 %”.

Lesson 4Structure: the Radial Distribution Function

The oxygen–oxygen radial distribution function is the best-measured structural property of water (from X-ray diffraction), so it is the most direct test of a model’s hydrogen-bond network. It is computed exactly as in Module 8, from a histogram of O···O distances, now averaged over 400 frames of the NVE run.

Our SPC/E run gives a first peak at 2.76 Å with height 3.04, a first minimum near 3.4 Å and a coordination number of 4.7 molecules in the first shell (counted to the first minimum). Compare with the LJ liquid of Module 8: its first peak is at the atomic diameter with 12 neighbours. In water the first peak is the hydrogen-bond distance, about 2.8 Å, with only four to five neighbours: an open, tetrahedral network. The experimental first peak is at about 2.8 Å and about 2.5 in height [3, 4]; SPC/E puts the peak at the right place but makes it clearly too tall (3.0 against 2.5: too structured), a typical result for fixed-charge models and the reason SPC/E’s first minimum is also too deep.

Lesson 5Dynamics: the Self-Diffusion Constant

As in Rahman’s argon, the Einstein relation gives the diffusion constant from the slope of the mean-squared displacement of the oxygens, $D = \lim_{t\to\infty}\mathrm{MSD}(t)/6t$. The curve below averages over all time origins of the 16 ps trajectory. At short times the molecules move ballistically (MSD $\propto t^2$) and then rattle in their hydrogen-bond cage; the diffusive, linear regime starts after about 1 ps, so we fit the slope between 1.5 and 5 ps (a longer run would allow a longer window).

The fit gives $D = $ 2.20 × 10⁻⁵ cm²/s. As an error estimate we split the run into two 8 ps blocks (slope between 1 and 3.5 ps): 2.21 ± 0.33, the two blocks giving 1.88 and 2.54. Two blocks cannot give a reliable error bar, but they tell us what matters: the uncertainty of $D$ from a 16 ps run is of order 15 %, not the few per cent that a single fit suggests.

The finite-size correction

With periodic boundaries a molecule is slowed by the hydrodynamic flow it induces in its own periodic images, so $D$ computed in a small box is too low. Yeh and Hummer [5] showed that the effect is known:

$$ D_\infty = D_\mathrm{PBC} + \frac{2.837\,k_BT}{6\pi\eta L}, $$

where $\eta$ is the shear viscosity and $L$ the box length. With the experimental viscosity of water at 25 °C (0.890 mPa s; the model’s own viscosity would be better, but we did not compute it) and $L = 15.54$ Å, the correction is +0.46 × 10⁻⁵ cm²/s, about 20 % of the experimental value, giving 2.66 × 10⁻⁵ cm²/s for the infinite system. The SPC/E literature value in the Vega–Abascal review is 2.54 × 10⁻⁵ cm²/s at 298 K, and the experimental value is 2.30 × 10⁻⁵ cm²/s [2, 5, 6]. Our corrected value is 16 % above experiment and 5 % above the literature: given a ±15 % uncertainty that is consistent with the literature. One more systematic effect is the temperature: our run averaged 304 K, and the diffusion constant of water rises by roughly 2–3 % per kelvin near room temperature, so about 6 K alone raises $D$ by ten per cent or more relative to 298 K. The finite-size correction matters: without it the uncorrected 125-molecule value looks 4 % below experiment, when the infinite-system value is above it.

Lesson 6The Dielectric Constant: a Slowly Converging Property

The static dielectric constant measures how strongly the liquid screens charges. In a periodic box with Ewald electrostatics (conducting, or “tin-foil”, boundary conditions) it follows from the fluctuations of the total dipole moment $\mathbf M = \sum_i q_i\mathbf r_i$ of the box:

$$ \varepsilon = 1 + \frac{4\pi}{3 V k_BT}\left(\langle \mathbf M^2\rangle - \langle\mathbf M\rangle^2\right). $$

This is a fluctuation formula: $\varepsilon$ is a measure of how much $\mathbf M$ wanders, and $\mathbf M$ wanders slowly because it involves the collective reorientation of all molecules. The reorientation of one molecule takes a few ps, but the dipole of the whole box decorrelates over tens to hundreds of ps in a box this size, so 16 ps is only a handful of independent samples, and the answer depends on whether a large slow excursion happens to occur. The whole run gives $\varepsilon = $ 77, which looks like an excellent agreement with the experimental 78.5, but it is a coincidence: the four 4 ps blocks give 10, 21, 11 and 9, and the cumulative estimate in the figure below is still rising at the end of the run. Vega and Abascal report 68 for SPC/E in Table 2 [2]; this trajectory does not converge to that model value. The only honest summary of our data is “not converged”.

A lesson in honesty

A single short run cannot say whether SPC/E underestimates the dielectric constant by 10 % or 20 %. The published comparison table gives 68 [2], but this 16 ps trajectory has not converged well enough to test that value. Properties that depend on collective motion (dielectric constant, viscosity, thermal conductivity) are much more expensive to converge than single-molecule ones (energy, structure, diffusion).

Lesson 7Pressure and Density

A direct way to compute the density of a model is an NPT simulation with a barostat. A cheaper check is to run at the experimental density (0.997 g/cm³) and compute the pressure: if the model’s equilibrium density at 1 bar is close to the experimental one, the pressure will be close to 1 bar. Our NVE run gives -21 ± 87 bar (standard error of four 4 ps blocks, which gave 155, 103, −179 and −161 bar). The pressure of liquid water fluctuates by hundreds of bar in a box of 125 molecules, and the isothermal compressibility of water is about 4.5 × 10⁻⁵ bar⁻¹, so an uncertainty of 100 bar corresponds to a density uncertainty of only about 0.5 %. The Vega–Abascal review quotes 0.994 g/cm³ for SPC/E at 298 K and 1 bar [2], which is 0.3 % below the experimental 0.997 and would correspond to a pressure of the order of 70 bar at 0.997 g/cm³. Our result is consistent with it and with 1 bar within the statistical noise: SPC/E at the experimental density is close to its own equilibrium, and the density is reproduced to better than the roughly 1 % that this run can resolve. (The pressure is computed from the derivative of the Ewald energy with respect to the volume, with the rigid molecules scaled as units; as a check of that routine, its dilute-gas limit reproduces the ideal-gas pressure.)

What do we conclude? SPC/E, simulated carefully, reproduces the energy and the heat of vaporisation (after the polarisation correction) to within about 1–2 % of experiment, puts the first peak of $g_\mathrm{OO}$ at the right distance but makes it too tall, and, with the finite-size correction, gives a diffusion constant within the uncertainty of our short run of the literature value (2.54) and about 15 % above experiment (the extra 6 K in our run contributes). The pressure at the experimental density is consistent with 1 bar. The dielectric constant is not determined by a 16 ps run: the number we got for the whole run is a statistical accident. Where our number disagrees with experiment, four things can be responsible, and a good simulator asks which:

  1. Finite size: 125 molecules (diffusion, dielectric constant, and every fluctuation property).
  2. Short runs: 16 ps (statistical error: the block estimates).
  3. Technical choices: cutoffs, the LJ tail correction, the time step, the thermostat.
  4. The model itself: a rigid, non-polarisable model with fixed charges, classical nuclei (Modules 10 and 11). This is the interesting one, and the one that only remains once the others are controlled.

You compute the diffusion constant of water in a box of 125 molecules and find a value 15 % below experiment. What should you check first?

  1. Whether the box is large enough: periodic images slow the molecule down and the Yeh–Hummer correction (about +20 % for this box) can account for most of the deficit.
  2. Whether the Lennard-Jones parameters are wrong.
  3. Whether the temperature was exactly 298.15 K: this alone gives a 15 % change.
Finite-size effects on diffusion are large and well understood: they scale as $1/L$, are about 20 % here, and are the first thing to correct before blaming the model. Temperature is a real effect too (D changes by about 2–3 % per kelvin near room temperature), but a 15 % deficit would require an error of several kelvin.

What you should see

(a) The heat of vaporisation and the position of the $g_\mathrm{OO}$ peak settle within a picosecond or two (they depend on single-molecule and pair averages that sample quickly). The diffusion constant needs the MSD to reach the linear regime (2–4 ps) and then several more picoseconds; its running estimate still drifts by 10–20 % at 10 ps. (b) $\Delta H_\mathrm{vap}$ hardly changes with the box size: the energy per molecule is a local quantity. $D$ is larger in the bigger box, by roughly the change in the Yeh–Hummer correction (it falls as $1/L$), which is the signature of a hydrodynamic finite-size effect rather than a modelling difference.

📝 Key Takeaways

  • Properties are averages over a trajectory; quote them with an uncertainty from independent blocks, and remember that the block error understates the true uncertainty of a short run.
  • $\Delta H_\mathrm{vap} = -\langle U\rangle/N + RT$ for a rigid model; effective models such as SPC/E also need the polarisation correction.
  • Single-molecule and local properties (energy, $g_\mathrm{OO}$) converge in a few ps; collective ones (the dielectric constant) take hundreds of ns.
  • Diffusion constants from periodic boxes need the Yeh–Hummer $1/L$ correction.
  • A comparison with experiment tests the model only after finite-size, run-length and technical errors have been controlled.
